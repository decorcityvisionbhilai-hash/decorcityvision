1. What Is This, and Why Does It Matter to You

Your website's text, photos, team members, service list, testimonials, and job postings are not hard-coded into the site anymore. They live in a separate, easy-to-use content system called Sanity — think of it as a private control panel for your website, similar in spirit to a WordPress admin panel, but built specifically around the way your site is designed today.

This means: whenever you want to change a phone number, add a new team member's photo, post a new job opening, or add a new project to your portfolio, you don't need to ask a developer to edit code and redeploy the site. You log into Sanity Studio, make the change, hit Publish, and it appears on the live site — usually within a few seconds, with no page refresh needed for a visitor already on the page.

Why this design choice Every piece of content in Sanity has a matching "fallback" value already written into the website's code. If you ever leave a field blank, delete a document, or something goes wrong with the connection, the site does not break or show blank space — it simply shows the last-known good content instead. You can experiment freely without fear of taking the site down.

This document walks through every single content type in your Studio, explains what each one controls on the live website, defines every field in plain language, and gives you step-by-step instructions for the changes you're most likely to make. Keep it as your reference — you shouldn't need a developer for day-to-day content updates after reading this.

2. Getting In: Two Ways to Reach the Studio

You can edit your content from either of these two places — they show the exact same content and editing screens, just hosted in two different places:

Option A — On Sanity's own hosting (recommended for day-to-day use)

Go to https://decorcityvision.sanity.studio in any browser and log in with the account you set up (Google, GitHub, or email/password — whichever you chose during setup). This is the simplest way to get in without remembering anything about the website's address.

Option B — Embedded inside the website itself

Go to https://decorcityvision.in/studio. This is the identical Studio, running as a page on your own website. Useful if you want to jump straight from browsing the live site into editing it.

Which one should I use? It doesn't matter — pick whichever is more convenient in the moment. Changes made in one show up through the other immediately, because they both edit the exact same underlying content.

3. The Golden Rule: Draft vs. Published, and Going Live

This is the single most important thing to understand about using the Studio.

When you open a document and start typing, Sanity automatically saves your work every few seconds — but only as a draft. A draft is invisible to visitors on your website. Nothing changes on the live site until you click the green Publish button, usually in the top-right corner of the document panel.

  1. Open the document you want to change from the menu on the left.
  2. Edit the field(s) you need — your typing auto-saves as a draft as you go, so you won't lose work if you close the tab by accident.
  3. Review your changes — scroll through the whole document once before publishing, especially if you touched several fields.
  4. Click Publish — this is the step that actually pushes your change live.

Once published, your website is set up to update live — any visitor who already has a page open will see the change appear automatically within a few seconds, with no need to refresh the page. This is powered by what's called Sanity's "Live Content API," which is already wired into every page.

Good to know If, for any reason, the live-update connection isn't active (for example, very old browsers, or a temporary network hiccup), the site is also set to automatically re-check for fresh content at least once every 60 seconds and on every new page visit. So worst case, a change is visible within a minute — it is never stuck showing old content indefinitely.

4. Finding Your Way Around the Studio

The left-hand menu in the Studio is organized in two parts:

Pinned at the top — "Settings" documents (only one of each exists)

  • Site Settings — your business's core facts: phone numbers, address, hours, social links, and more.
  • Hero — the big homepage banner at the very top of the site.
  • Budget Slider Settings — the min/max/default values on the contact form's budget slider.

These three are called singletons — there is exactly one of each, always. You cannot create a second "Site Settings" document, and you should never delete these three. If a field inside one of them feels intimidating, remember: leaving it blank simply keeps the website's built-in default for that one field (see the box below for the one exception).

How blank fields behave For almost every field across the whole Studio: if you leave it blank, the website quietly falls back to a sensible default that's already built into the code — it will never show up blank or broken on the live site. This applies even to grouped fields like Site Settings' "Studio Visiting Hours" — you can fill in just the days and leave the opening/closing time blank, and those two will still correctly show their defaults rather than disappearing.

Below the divider — everything else (repeatable lists)

Below the three pinned settings, you'll find a list of content types where you can have many documents — Team Members, Projects, Gallery Images, Testimonials, and so on. Click a type in the menu to see the list of existing documents, then click + Create new (usually top-right) to add another, or click an existing item to edit it.

Most of these lists have an Order field (a plain number). The website displays these lists sorted by that number, smallest first. Use 0, 1, 2, 3… to control exactly what order things appear in on the website — this is the main way you control sequencing anywhere in the Studio.

21. Common Tasks, Step by Step

Add a new team member

  1. Open Team Members in the left menu, click + Create new.
  2. Fill in their real Name and Role.
  3. Click the Photo field and upload a real photo of them.
  4. Set an Order number one higher than your current last team member (e.g. if you have 6 members numbered 0–5, use 6).
  5. Click Publish.

Add a new gallery photo, including a brand-new category

  1. Open Gallery Images, click + Create new.
  2. Give it a short Title and upload the Image.
  3. In Category, type a name — reuse an existing one (Kitchen, Bedroom, etc.) or type something brand new like "Custom Work."
  4. Set an Order number.
  5. Click Publish — the new category's filter pill appears on the site automatically.

Post a new job opening

  1. Open Job Openings, click + Create new.
  2. Fill in Title, Department, Location, and Type.
  3. Write a short Description and add a few Requirements as separate list items.
  4. Leave Active switched on.
  5. Click Publish.

When the role is filled, open the document again and switch active off, then Publish — this removes it from the careers page without losing the content, in case you want to reopen it later.

Add your Instagram and Facebook links

  1. Open Site Settings.
  2. Scroll to Social links.
  3. Paste your full Instagram profile URL and/or Facebook page URL.
  4. Click Publish — the icons appear in the footer immediately; whichever one you leave blank simply won't show an icon.

Switch the homepage hero from a photo to a video

  1. Open Hero.
  2. Set "Expanding panel media type" to video.
  3. Upload your video file to the "Panel video" field that appears (keep it small — see Section 6's warning).
  4. Optionally upload a "Video poster frame" still image.
  5. Click Publish.

Update your studio's visiting hours

  1. Open Site Settings → Studio Visiting Hours.
  2. Update Days, Open time, and/or Close time — you can fill in just one and leave the others as they are.
  3. Click Publish.

22. Good Habits and Things to Avoid

  • Always click Publish. A saved draft is invisible to visitors — this trips people up more than anything else.
  • Never delete Site Settings, Hero, or Budget Slider Settings. These three must always exist exactly once. If one is ever accidentally deleted, the site falls back to its built-in defaults everywhere that document was used, but it's best avoided.
  • Keep Order numbers tidy. You don't need to renumber everything every time — gaps are fine (0, 5, 10) — just make sure the numbers sort in the order you actually want.
  • Only real content for Testimonials and Team Members. See the honesty notes in Sections 13 and 14 — this is a trust issue, not just a style preference.
  • Compress large photos before uploading when possible. Sanity handles resizing automatically for display, but a very large original (10MB+) still takes longer to upload and process.
  • Use the "active" toggle instead of deleting for job openings you might reopen later — it's reversible, deleting isn't.
  • When in doubt, leave it blank. Every field is designed to fall back gracefully to a safe default rather than break the site — you cannot break the live site by leaving something empty.

23. Troubleshooting

"I published a change, but I can't see it on the website"

  • Confirm you clicked the green Publish button, not just closed the tab (a draft alone won't show).
  • Hard-refresh the page (Ctrl/Cmd + Shift + R) — this forces the browser to fetch the freshest version.
  • Wait up to 60 seconds — the site's safety-net refresh cycle guarantees it shows up within that window even in the rare case the instant live-update didn't fire.

"An image isn't showing on the site"

  • Open the document and confirm the image actually finished uploading (you should see a preview thumbnail in the field, not a spinner or blank box).
  • Confirm you clicked Publish after uploading.

"A job/gallery photo/team member I deleted is still showing"

  • In Sanity, deleting a document removes it immediately — if you still see it, it's very likely a caching/refresh issue. Hard-refresh the page.

"I see a weird error in the Studio itself"

  • Try logging out and back in.
  • If it persists, take a screenshot of the exact error message and send it to Googlixlabs — most Studio-level errors are configuration issues that take minutes to fix, not content problems you caused.
